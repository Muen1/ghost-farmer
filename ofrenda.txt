Ah, beans. A reliable companion. Sow them in late winter, or early spring.  Choose a spot with good drainage, like a slightly damp area.  They need a good, steady supply of rain, but don’t let the ground dry out completely.  Don’t over-fertilize, it can be a stubborn crop.  I remember my grandfather always said, "A happy bean is a good harvest."

Right now, after the last frost, is a good time.  Wait until the soil temperature is consistently warm – around 60 degrees Fahrenheit – before planting.  Don’t be afraid to give it a little extra time – it’s a patient crop.  A little frost is good, but it’s best to wait until the ground is workable.  Trust your instincts, and let the soil guide you.

Hmm, aphids. A bothersome nuisance.  First, gently spray with a strong stream of water.  Then, consider a spray of neem oil – it’s a natural deterrent.  If that doesn’t work, hand-picking can be effective.  Keep the soil damp – aphids hate wet feet.  And remember, a strong, fresh breeze will often drive them away.  It was a dreadful infestation during my youth, you see, with those little green devils.
